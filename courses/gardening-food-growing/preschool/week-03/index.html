WEEK 3 · DAY 1 · UNIT 03 · LESSON 11

📝 Plan a Little Garden

📝 🪴 🌈

Our growing project can be a real adult-supervised container, a paper garden or a picture journal. We choose something possible for our home, mobility, materials and local season. The goal is to show safe thinking, not to grow the biggest plant.

Try with a grown-up: Draw a project with one plant picture, one safe place and a grown-up helper.

Talk together: What makes this project right for your family?

WEEK 3 · DAY 2 · UNIT 03 · LESSON 12

🎨 Build the Garden Model

🎨 🌱 📦

A model helps us share an idea before using real supplies. Paper flowers, drawn pots and pretend watering drops are safe options. A grown-up must approve any real seeds, plants or soil. We do not touch sharp tools or eat unknown leaves.

Try with a grown-up: Create three paper garden parts, or draw them, then put them in order.

Talk together: What are the three parts you chose?

WEEK 3 · DAY 3 · UNIT 03 · LESSON 13

📊 Notice a Change

📊 👀 🌻

A garden diary tells what we saw, not what we hoped would happen. Plants can grow slowly; a paper model can show an imagined change clearly labeled as pretend. When something changes, draw it or tell an adult. It is fine if nothing changes.

Try with a grown-up: Make two pictures labeled before and after, or today and another day.

Talk together: What is observed, and what is imagined?

WEEK 3 · DAY 4 · UNIT 03 · LESSON 14

🔄 Improve Our Plan

🔄 ☀️ 🪴

Gardeners learn by making gentle changes. The place may be too dark or a pretend picture journal may be hard to follow. Ask a grown-up what would be safer or clearer. We can make our drawing easier to read without buying anything.

Try with a grown-up: Ask a grown-up for one suggestion and change your paper plan.

Talk together: What did you improve and why?

WEEK 3 · DAY 5 · UNIT 03 · LESSON 15

🌼 Share Our Garden Story

🌼 🎉 🌱

Show a grown-up your model or garden journal. Point to water, light, a safe growing place and one safety rule. Tell what you noticed, what you practiced and what is still uncertain. The adult documents what they observed. Knowledge scores alone do not certify garden skills.

Try with a grown-up: Present the project, describe three needs or safety ideas, and record adult feedback.

Talk together: What would you try during another growing season?
