DAY 1 · UNIT 01 · LESSON 01

💡 What Is a Helpful Idea

💡 🖼️ 💛

An entrepreneur notices a problem and imagines a way to help. A preschool idea might be a colorful book label or a pretend toy organizer. An idea does not need money or a real business. We can draw it, ask a caregiver what is safe, and think about whom it could help.

Try together: Draw a pretend tool that helps organize picture books.

Talk together: Who might benefit from your idea?

DAY 2 · UNIT 01 · LESSON 02

💡 Notice a Need

💡 🖼️ 💛

A need is something important such as safety, food, shelter or care. A want is something we may enjoy but can live without. People have different needs, and we should not assume everyone's family has the same resources. Noticing carefully and asking kindly can guide a helpful idea.

Try together: Sort drawings of drinking water, a cozy toy and a safe crossing into needs and wants.

Talk together: How can we ask what someone needs respectfully?

DAY 3 · UNIT 01 · LESSON 03

💡 Imagine a Product

💡 🖼️ 💛

A product is an item designed for someone to use. In our pretend workshop, a paper bookmark could be a product. Children only use safe paper and crayons with caregiver supervision; sharp tools and real sales are not required. A useful product should be accessible and easy to understand.

Try together: Draw a bookmark with large symbols that different readers can recognize.

Talk together: How would your drawing help someone use the product?

DAY 4 · UNIT 01 · LESSON 04

💡 Kind Services

💡 🖼️ 💛

A service means doing something helpful for someone. Reading a story or offering to tidy safe toys can be pretend services. Children should not perform adult jobs or help unfamiliar people alone. Asking permission and respecting boundaries matter more than earning pretend coins.

Try together: Match a picture of a service, such as reading together, with the person it might help.

Talk together: Why do we ask before helping?

DAY 5 · UNIT 01 · LESSON 05

💡 Share a Pretend Pitch

💡 🖼️ 💛

A pitch is a short explanation of an idea. We can show a drawing, name a need and explain how our imaginary product or service helps. Listening to questions is part of presenting. No one must post a video, share a real address or ask anyone to buy something.

Try together: Tell a caregiver your idea in three pictures: need, idea and who it helps.

Talk together: How could someone improve your idea?
