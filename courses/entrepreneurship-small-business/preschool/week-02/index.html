DAY 6 · UNIT 02 · LESSON 06

💡 Design a Picture Sign

💡 🖼️ 💛

Signs help people understand where something belongs or what an imaginary activity offers. Large symbols and readable words can make signs more accessible. Our market sign is pretend, so it does not advertise a real business or ask for purchases.

Try together: Draw a shop sign for pretend bookmarks using large pictures.

Talk together: What makes a sign easy to read?

DAY 7 · UNIT 02 · LESSON 07

💡 Choose Safe Materials

💡 🖼️ 💛

A project plan should use safe, available materials. Paper, crayons or reusable picture cards can work without spending money. Caregivers check materials before children handle them. We never use hot glue, cutting machines, real money or unknown tools for this exercise.

Try together: Choose safe drawing materials from a picture assortment.

Talk together: Who checks materials for safety?

DAY 8 · UNIT 02 · LESSON 08

💡 Listen to a Customer

💡 🖼️ 💛

A customer is someone interested in a product or service. In a pretend conversation, we can ask what a person finds useful and listen to the answer. People have different preferences, so a kind maker does not pressure anyone to buy. Children never contact real strangers for this lesson.

Try together: Role-play a friend asking for a larger bookmark drawing.

Talk together: How can we respond kindly to a different preference?

DAY 9 · UNIT 02 · LESSON 09

💡 Compare Pretend Prices

💡 🖼️ 💛

A price tells how much something costs. We can practice comparing two pretend labels with drawn dots or tokens. Two dots are fewer than four dots, but value and fairness are more than a number. No real money, transactions or financial records are needed.

Try together: Count two paper-token labels with 2 and 4 dots and identify which has fewer.

Talk together: Which pretend price shows fewer tokens?

DAY 10 · UNIT 02 · LESSON 10

💡 Work as a Team

💡 🖼️ 💛

Different teammates may draw, organize, explain or check a plan. Taking turns and asking for help makes the project more welcoming. People communicate in different ways, and everyone deserves an accessible role. Rest breaks and safe adult supervision are part of good teamwork.

Try together: Share pretend project roles using picture cards and switch turns.

Talk together: How can a team include someone who uses gestures?
