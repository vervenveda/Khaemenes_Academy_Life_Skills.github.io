DAY 11 · UNIT 03 · LESSON 11

💞 My Friendship Plan

💞 🖼️ 💛

A friendship plan can show respectful invitations, listening, shared choices and personal boundaries. Friends can have different interests, and no one is required to be friends with everyone. We can be respectful even when we prefer separate activities.

Try together: Draw three cards: invite kindly, ask permission, and respect a choice.

Talk together: What makes a friendship feel safe and welcoming?

DAY 12 · UNIT 03 · LESSON 12

💞 Practice Listening

💞 🖼️ 💛

Good listening means noticing what someone communicates, not simply waiting to talk. A partner may use words, gestures or pictures. We can ask 'Did I understand?' and allow time for a response. We never force another person to share private feelings.

Try together: Practice a picture dialogue and reflect the other person's preference.

Talk together: How do we check whether we understood?

DAY 13 · UNIT 03 · LESSON 13

💞 Choose a Helpful Response

💞 🖼️ 💛

When someone is sad, angry or uncomfortable, we can offer a kind response without assuming what they want. Ask whether they would like space, help or a trusted adult. Children are not responsible for fixing everyone else's emotions. Safety and boundaries come first.

Try together: Choose from three picture responses to a pretend friend asking for space.

Talk together: What could we say before trying to help?

DAY 14 · UNIT 03 · LESSON 14

💞 Revise My Kindness Plan

💞 🖼️ 💛

Plans improve with feedback. A picture sign might need larger symbols or another language; a group activity may need quiet space. A trusted adult can help us make changes. Respecting a person's 'no' remains important even when our original idea was kind.

Try together: Ask a caregiver for one accessibility suggestion and redraw your plan.

Talk together: How did the revision include more people?

DAY 15 · UNIT 03 · LESSON 15

💞 My Kind Communication Showcase

💞 🖼️ 💛

Share an illustrated story showing a feeling, a respectful response, permission or a boundary, and one way to ask for help. Explain what you learned in words, drawings, signs or an accessible device. A caregiver records observed participation separately from the knowledge check. The activity never requires disclosure of personal experiences.

Try together: Present a fictional friendship story to your caregiver and reflect on a safe choice.

Talk together: What would you do if someone's boundary was ignored?
