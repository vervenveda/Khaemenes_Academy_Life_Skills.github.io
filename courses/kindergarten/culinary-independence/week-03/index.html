Day 1: Design a Safe Snack

Choose a realistic project with adult approval.

Imagine a simple snack or pretend meal made from paper images. Plan how a caregiver would check allergies and choose safe materials. No cooking is required for assessment. List what the child can do and what only the adult may do.

My evidence: Sketch a pretend snack and label two safe child roles.

Day 2: Count the Recipe

Represent ingredient amounts using pictures.

Create a recipe card with one, two or three pretend scoops or items. Count aloud and match numerals to pictures. Compare which pictured ingredient needs more. An empty measuring cup is fine; do not prepare actual food without an adult.

My evidence: Draw a three-item recipe card with accurate quantities.

Day 3: Kitchen Team Roles

Practice cooperating and requesting help.

Someone can draw instructions, another can count paper scoops, and a grown-up makes real kitchen safety decisions. Asking permission and respecting an answer are important skills. Different learners can participate by pointing, speaking, signing or drawing.

My evidence: Draw three helpers and identify one respectful request.

Day 4: Improve My Recipe

Revise your plan after feedback.

Show a trusted caregiver your picture plan. Ask if the instructions are easy to follow, the food choice is suitable, and the child/adult roles are safe. Revise one step based on that feedback. A change is evidence of learning, not a failure.

My evidence: Show a before-and-after picture with one safety improvement.

Day 5: Kitchen Explorer Celebration

Present a final, accessible family portfolio.

Present five lesson pictures or notes and a finished three-to-five-step snack plan. Explain how you count and sequence, why an adult checks allergies, and why hot tools stay adult-only. A quiz is a knowledge check; it does not prove hands-on kitchen competence.

My evidence: Prepare your final printable picture recipe and reflect on one learned safety rule.
