Day 1: Kitchen Safety Detectives

Spot safe and unsafe kitchen choices.

Look at a pretend kitchen picture or draw one. Find a sink, a stove, a clean table and a sharp tool. A kindergarten kitchen detective knows that hot pans, knives, appliances and cleaning chemicals are for responsible adults. Children can help by washing hands, identifying hazards and asking before touching.

My evidence: Draw two safe choices and circle one item a grown-up handles.

Day 2: Clean Hands, Ready Foods

Sequence five steps for clean hands.

Before handling food, use soap and water: wet hands, add soap, rub all surfaces for about 20 seconds, rinse and dry. Ask a caregiver to help if the sink is difficult to reach. A paper handwashing sequence can be practiced without running water.

My evidence: Number five picture steps in order and explain why soap matters.

Day 3: Rainbow Food Sort

Classify foods and notice variety.

Foods come in many colors and forms. Use drawings or pretend food cards to sort fruits, vegetables and other foods. Colors are clues for observing, not proof that a food is healthy or safe for every person. Families have different traditions and dietary needs; adults check allergies.

My evidence: Draw three different foods and sort them by a chosen feature.

Day 4: Measure and Count

Compare quantities using pretend scoops.

In a pretend recipe, count one scoop, two scoops and three scoops. A full scoop and half scoop are different amounts. We can compare amounts using dry paper shapes or empty cups—no hot equipment or real ingredients needed. If preparing actual food, a trusted adult chooses and supervises tools.

My evidence: Draw two cups and label which holds more pretend scoops.

Day 5: My Three-Step Snack Plan

Create and explain an adult-approved picture recipe.

A recipe is a sequence. Plan three steps for a pretend snack: choose suitable food, ask an adult to prepare anything requiring a knife or heat, and enjoy or serve safely. Real food handling requires caregiver approval, handwashing and allergy checks. Families can replace the pretend food with paper cards.

My evidence: Draw three numbered recipe pictures and explain one safety rule.
