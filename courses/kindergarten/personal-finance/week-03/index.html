Day 1: Plan a Mini Market

Create a paper market with three pretend products.

Use drawings and clear pretend prices. No actual sales, payment links or accounts.

Example: A mini-market is a picture-based game with fictional prices. The shop might include three drawn products and a sign explaining that tokens are pretend.

Try it together: Draft three product cards and arrange them in a clear layout.

My independent picture challenge: Create a paper market sign with item names and token prices from one to five.

Caregiver teaching note: No payment links, sales, real accounts or requests for contact information are permitted.

My evidence: Draw three items and their token costs.

Day 2: Check My Budget

Compare total tokens with a planned purchase.

Start with five tokens. Count the pretend price of each item and decide whether enough remain.

Example: With five tokens, a three-token picture purchase leaves two tokens. Two items costing three each would exceed five.

Try it together: Compare two purchase combinations, add their imaginary prices and decide which fits a five-token budget.

My independent picture challenge: Draw five tokens, cross out the imagined spending and show the remainder.

Caregiver teaching note: Prioritize counting and reasoning over buying behavior; allow spoken, pictorial or tactile responses.

My evidence: Write a picture equation.

Day 3: Kind Choices and Fairness

Recognize different needs and abilities.

Design signs that are readable and welcome different families. Respect people who choose not to buy.

Example: A fair classroom market welcomes people who communicate differently or have different needs. Large price labels and pictures can make choices clearer.

Try it together: Compare a tiny-print price card with a clear large-number card, then discuss which is easier to understand.

My independent picture challenge: Design a welcoming store poster with large labels and a respectful message.

Caregiver teaching note: Never rank people by purchasing power; adapt for visual and motor access, and respect those who choose not to participate.

My evidence: Draw a welcoming market sign.

Day 4: Improve the Market

Ask a caregiver to review clarity and safety.

Review whether the picture prices make sense and whether instructions protect private information. Revise one feature.

Example: A reviewer may notice that a three-token price label is too small. Making the symbol larger improves the project.

Try it together: Ask a caregiver which details are readable and whether the market uses only fictional information.

My independent picture challenge: Draw a before-and-after version of a clearer price tag or shop rule.

Caregiver teaching note: Keep criticism kind and practical. Maintain the learner's ownership of creative decisions.

My evidence: Create a before-and-after example.

Day 5: Finance Learning Gallery

Present a paper portfolio and reflection.

Share needs/wants, counting and saving pictures, a mock budget and a kind choice. The quiz does not certify financial competence.

Example: A learning gallery may show a needs-and-wants card, a counting example, a saving chart and a revised mini-market. These show growth rather than earnings.

Try it together: Present one budget choice and one considerate decision, explaining what changed with feedback.

My independent picture challenge: Make a portfolio cover with Academy title, grade, date, sample equation and a learning reflection.

Caregiver teaching note: Use a learner code rather than personal financial details. Completion does not authorize financial transactions.

My evidence: Create a final paper market poster.
