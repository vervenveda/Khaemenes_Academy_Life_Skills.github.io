Day 1: Saving for Later

Understand saving with pretend tokens.

Set aside picture tokens today for a planned pretend choice later. Saving is a planning skill, not a measure of a family's worth.

Example: Saving is setting aside resources for something later. In a picture game, keeping two out of five tokens leaves three to use now.

Try it together: Separate five drawn tokens into save and use groups and count both groups together.

My independent picture challenge: Draw a saving jar with two pretend tokens and write or show what they are for.

Caregiver teaching note: Saving habits are learning skills, not a judgment of family circumstances; children do not control household money.

My evidence: Draw tokens in save and use piles.

Day 2: Counting Change

Practice addition and subtraction with paper tokens.

Two tokens plus one is three; three minus one is two. Count drawings, not real money.

Example: Two picture tokens plus one is three. If one of those three is used in a pretend purchase, two remain.

Try it together: Build two addition stories and one subtraction story with movable paper circles.

My independent picture challenge: Draw an equation using five or fewer pretend tokens and explain it in your preferred format.

Caregiver teaching note: Use tactile cards, speech or signs as alternatives to writing; no real money is necessary.

My evidence: Draw an addition and subtraction token story.

Day 3: Compare Choices

Make a choice with reasons.

Consider usefulness, safety and your pretend budget. There may be more than one thoughtful answer.

Example: A child might choose a picture notebook over an extra decoration because it will be used more often. Another learner might reasonably choose differently.

Try it together: Compare two fictional products for usefulness, pretend cost and personal preferences. Discuss more than one thoughtful answer.

My independent picture challenge: Draw two options and mark the one you choose, giving one reason.

Caregiver teaching note: Respect different family needs and avoid presenting a single preference as morally superior.

My evidence: Draw two choices and one reason.

Day 4: Giving and Sharing

Practice kindness with consent.

Sharing is a choice; always ask before giving away something that belongs to someone else. Helping need not involve money.

Example: Sharing could mean offering a drawing or helping someone clean up paper cards. Giving away another person's possessions requires permission.

Try it together: Role-play asking 'May I share this?' and accepting either yes or no. Identify a kind action that costs nothing.

My independent picture challenge: Draw a sharing-with-consent scene and a non-money way to help.

Caregiver teaching note: Sharing is voluntary, and children should never feel pressured to donate money or belongings.

My evidence: Draw a non-money way to help.

Day 5: My Saving Picture Plan

Design a safe goal chart.

Make a five-box chart with paper icons for steps toward a pretend goal. Adults control real purchases.

Example: A saving chart with five boxes can show pretend steps toward a picture-book goal; each completed practice step earns one drawn star, not actual currency.

Try it together: Count five goal boxes and discuss which steps are realistic and safe.

My independent picture challenge: Design a five-part pretend saving journey with labels and one caregiver check.

Caregiver teaching note: Adults supervise financial decisions. Do not request real purchase plans or private balances.

My evidence: Draw a five-step saving chart.
