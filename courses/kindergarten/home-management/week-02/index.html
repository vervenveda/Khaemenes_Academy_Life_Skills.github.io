Day 1: Clothes and Weather

Match picture clothes to different weather conditions.

Use paper drawings to plan clothing for imaginary weather. A caregiver decides real outdoor clothing and safety.

Example: A rainy-day drawing might include a raincoat, while a sunny-day picture may include a brimmed hat. Clothing varies with climate and accessibility.

Try it together: Match three weather cards with three clothing drawings. Explain one choice and say who checks the actual forecast.

My independent picture challenge: Make a two-column pretend weather-and-clothing chart.

Caregiver teaching note: Caregivers determine real outdoor conditions; accommodate sensory preferences, cultural clothing and individual needs.

My evidence: Draw two clothing choices for two pretend weather days.

Day 2: Laundry Safety Detective

Recognize safe and unsafe laundry activities.

Young children can pair pictures of clean socks. Detergent, washer and dryer controls are adult-only; never climb inside an appliance.

Example: A laundry symbol may say gentle wash or do not tumble dry. Children can learn symbols without operating a washing machine.

Try it together: Examine drawn tags and place picture socks into matching color groups. Name one item an adult handles.

My independent picture challenge: Draw a safe laundry sorting card and a symbol meaning ask an adult.

Caregiver teaching note: Washing products, machines, electricity and hot irons are adult-only; no independent operation is required.

My evidence: Draw a safe sock matching game.

Day 3: Make a Picture Checklist

Create a simple four-step home routine.

A checklist turns a task into smaller steps. Choose a safe routine such as putting paper cards away with adult approval.

Example: A checklist helps us remember a short task: choose, do, check. Some people use stickers or pictures instead of words.

Try it together: Build a three-box checklist for putting paper art away, then identify which box comes first.

My independent picture challenge: Create a reusable paper checklist with two achievable steps and one final review box.

Caregiver teaching note: Provide large boxes and alternatives to writing; do not insist on completion speed.

My evidence: Number four safe picture steps.

Day 4: Sharing Home Responsibilities

Listen to others and respect different abilities.

Families divide work differently. No child is responsible for adult health, dangerous housework, or another person's personal care.

Example: Fair sharing does not mean every household member does the same task. Age, ability, time and need can change how people contribute.

Try it together: Compare two pretend helpers: one reads the labels and one draws the pictures. Describe how both contribute.

My independent picture challenge: Draw a teamwork scene and add a speech bubble asking for help.

Caregiver teaching note: Avoid assigning chores by gender; caregiver approval and accessible contributions come first.

My evidence: Show two ways family members can cooperate.

Day 5: My Tidy Corner Map

Design an accessible paper organization plan.

Draw a shelf, toy bin and safe pathway. Plan without moving furniture or using tools. Ask whether everyone can reach the pictured supplies.

Example: A map can show where a book, puzzle or paper craft belongs. A label or picture may help everyone find it again.

Try it together: Use three paper item cards and draw where each belongs on a simple map. Explain your organization choices.

My independent picture challenge: Create a 'before' and 'after' diagram of a pretend tidy corner.

Caregiver teaching note: A corner may be shared or temporary. Keep pathways clear; no child should move large furniture.

My evidence: Draw a labeled corner map.
