Day 1: Choose a Helping Project

Select an age-safe challenge.

Plan a project using paper and pictures: organize pretend cards, create a family routine chart or design a safe toy shelf map. Ask for caregiver approval.

Example: A project has a goal and a plan. A picture-card organizer might help locate favorite activities without buying shelves.

Try it together: Choose one paper-only project, name its goal, list two steps and identify one adult permission point.

My independent picture challenge: Draw a goal box, materials symbols and a safe finish line.

Caregiver teaching note: Approve a manageable project; honor learners who demonstrate understanding without physically completing a chore.

My evidence: Draw the project and one adult safety role.

Day 2: Count and Label

Use numbers and symbols to organize.

Choose one to five pretend items, label groups, and compare totals. Avoid real lifting or handling unknown materials.

Example: Counting labels helps check our work. For example, four picture books can be grouped as two plus two.

Try it together: Count five paper cards, draw two groups and show that both groups together make five.

My independent picture challenge: Create labels 1 through 5 or equivalent tactile/visual marks for a pretend shelf.

Caregiver teaching note: Offer concrete counters or verbal counting. Adults handle any actual sharp labeling tools.

My evidence: Draw and count two groups.

Day 3: Make a Friendly Reminder

Create a helpful and respectful sign.

Make a sign reminding people to wash hands, put safe supplies back, or ask permission. Accessible pictures help everyone.

Example: A useful reminder is short, kind and specific: 'Books here, please' is clearer than 'Be better.'

Try it together: Compare a kind prompt with a vague one. Ask which is more helpful and why.

My independent picture challenge: Draw a large icon and compose one positive reminder in the learner's preferred communication mode.

Caregiver teaching note: Use readable contrast and large icons; a reminder should not shame a child or visitor.

My evidence: Draw a positive reminder sign.

Day 4: Feedback and Improvement

Revise a plan with caregiver guidance.

Ask a trusted adult whether the project is safe, clear and welcoming. Adjust one picture or step. Improvement is a valuable part of learning.

Example: Feedback describes how the work functions. Someone might say, 'The arrow is hard to see'; the artist could make it larger.

Try it together: Ask a trusted reviewer what part is clear and what could improve, then choose one appropriate change.

My independent picture challenge: Draw a before-and-after arrow or label and describe why it was adjusted.

Caregiver teaching note: Invite but do not force revisions. Praise effort and decision-making instead of demanding perfection.

My evidence: Draw a before-and-after improvement.

Day 5: Family Helper Showcase

Present and reflect on a complete plan.

Present five practice records and a final safe plan. Explain what you can do, where adults help, and how you respect other people's choices.

Example: A showcase is a chance to explain what was learned. A learner might present a map, checklist and friendly sign.

Try it together: Practice describing the goal, one safe choice and one change. Listeners can ask a respectful question.

My independent picture challenge: Create a portfolio cover with project title, date, lesson, an explanation and caregiver review.

Caregiver teaching note: Allow speech, gesture, pictures or typed notes. Preserve private household details and never photograph personal addresses.

My evidence: Share a final picture portfolio and one new skill.
