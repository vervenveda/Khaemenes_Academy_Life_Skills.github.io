Day 1: Plan a Healthy Day

Arrange safe habits in a daily picture plan.

Choose morning, daytime and nighttime routines suitable for your family and abilities with caregiver guidance.

Example: A visual routine could show a morning wash, an afternoon movement break and an evening rest activity. Schedules vary by family.

Try it together: Order three habit pictures and explain a safe adult-supported choice for each.

My independent picture challenge: Draw a morning–daytime–evening plan with symbols and flexible timing.

Caregiver teaching note: Avoid rigid or shame-based routines; support learners with different access and energy needs.

My evidence: Draw three times of day.

Day 2: Safe Choice Sorting

Sort child-friendly and adult-only tasks.

Health habits like washing hands are child-friendly with guidance; medicines and emergency care require adults and professionals.

Example: Some jobs are child-friendly with guidance, such as drawing a handwashing poster. Handling medicine or treating injuries belongs to adults.

Try it together: Sort six imaginary cards into 'practice with guidance' and 'ask an adult or professional'.

My independent picture challenge: Create two labeled safety boxes and explain one item in each.

Caregiver teaching note: Do not authorize actual procedures from game results; keep every activity illustrative and supervised.

My evidence: Make two labeled paper-card groups.

Day 3: Respect and Boundaries

Practice kindness and personal boundaries.

Ask permission before touching other people or their belongings. Everyone can say no to unwanted touch and seek a trusted adult.

Example: Asking before borrowing, helping or touching someone respects choice. Someone can say no to a hug even when offered kindly.

Try it together: Practice a fictional 'May I?' question and an accepting response to yes or no.

My independent picture challenge: Draw a two-panel scene that shows asking and respecting a boundary.

Caregiver teaching note: Reinforce bodily autonomy and never compel touch or explanations of private experiences.

My evidence: Draw a kind permission dialogue.

Day 4: Revise a Help Plan

Improve your drawing after caregiver review.

Ask whether safety steps are clear. Don't include private phone numbers or medical records in public school pages.

Example: A good help plan shows which kind of adult to seek and which actions to avoid. Feedback might reveal that a symbol is unclear.

Try it together: Invite a caregiver to review a fictional plan for clarity and local relevance; identify one useful change.

My independent picture challenge: Draw before-and-after versions of a help symbol or sequence.

Caregiver teaching note: Keep home addresses and medical details off public or shareable classroom evidence.

My evidence: Draw a before-and-after change.

Day 5: Wellbeing Portfolio Day

Present safe knowledge without medical simulation.

Share picture cards for handwashing, feelings, asking for help and the final daily plan. Quiz passage does not authorize independent first aid.

Example: A learning portfolio can show handwashing steps, feelings cards, a trusted-helper diagram and a safety choice poster. It demonstrates knowledge, not professional skill.

Try it together: Practice describing one new habit, one safe choice and one time to seek adult help.

My independent picture challenge: Make a portfolio cover with Academy heading, course, date and a short reflection.

Caregiver teaching note: Celebrate thoughtful learning. Quiz completion never grants medical or first-aid certification.

My evidence: Create a final labeled poster.
