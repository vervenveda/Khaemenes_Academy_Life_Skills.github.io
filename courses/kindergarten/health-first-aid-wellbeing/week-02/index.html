Day 1: Feelings Are Information

Name feelings using pictures.

People may feel glad, worried, upset or frustrated. Feelings are not wrong; ask a trusted adult for help.

Example: A face may look surprised, worried or happy, but expressions do not always reveal what someone feels. Asking respectfully is better than guessing.

Try it together: Look at four fictional faces and suggest possible feelings without claiming certainty.

My independent picture challenge: Draw feeling cards and a sentence, gesture or symbol for asking an adult for support.

Caregiver teaching note: Do not require personal disclosure; feelings are valid and adults should respond without ridicule.

My evidence: Draw four expression cards.

Day 2: People I Can Ask

Practice finding trusted adults.

If you are frightened, hurt or lost, seek a trusted adult. If one isn't available, find another safe helper.

Example: A trusted helper may be a caregiver, teacher or another adult the family identifies. If one cannot help, a child can seek another appropriate adult.

Try it together: Role-play saying or pointing to 'I need help' on a paper card; discuss which adults are safe to approach.

My independent picture challenge: Draw a private helper circle with roles rather than real names or contact information.

Caregiver teaching note: Agree on helpers with the family. Never publish contact details or encourage trusting unknown online adults.

My evidence: Draw a safe helper plan without addresses.

Day 3: Tell an Adult About Injuries

Understand age-appropriate first-aid boundaries.

Stop, move away from danger and call a trusted adult if anyone is injured. Never handle blood, medicines or sharp objects without responsible adults.

Example: If someone is hurt, a child can stop, get to a safe place and tell a responsible adult. Medical care decisions belong to adults and professionals.

Try it together: Sort fictional cards: tell an adult, touch blood, use unknown medicine. Explain the safe choice.

My independent picture challenge: Create a three-panel stop–safe place–tell picture strip.

Caregiver teaching note: Do not rehearse contact with injuries, blood or medicines. For serious emergencies follow local professional guidance.

My evidence: Draw an ask-for-help card.

Day 4: Emergency Helper Practice

Role-play a safe help request.

Emergency numbers differ worldwide. A caregiver can teach the correct local number. Do not place practice calls to real emergency services.

Example: Emergency phone numbers differ by location. An adult can explain how to get help and what information emergency responders may ask for.

Try it together: Use a pretend paper telephone to practice a clear request without dialing any numbers.

My independent picture challenge: Draw a help-request card with problem, adult helper and location symbol, but no real address.

Caregiver teaching note: Never place practice calls to emergency services; caregivers teach locally accurate procedures.

My evidence: Draw three pieces of information for a pretend call.

Day 5: My Calm Corner

Make a quiet-support plan.

A calm corner can have a favorite drawing, slow breathing and a plan to contact an adult. Everyone may use different support strategies.

Example: A calm place may have comfortable seating, a favorite picture and a way to contact an adult. Different people find different strategies helpful.

Try it together: Choose two calming picture cards and one asking-for-help card for a fictional scenario.

My independent picture challenge: Draw a three-step plan: notice, choose comfort, seek support.

Caregiver teaching note: Calming activities are not a substitute for help when safety is at risk; offer accessible sensory choices.

My evidence: Draw a calm corner with three features.
