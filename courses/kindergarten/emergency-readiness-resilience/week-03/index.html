Day 1: Design a Safe Help Plan

Draw a trusted-helper card and an approved safe-place symbol; leave out private contact information.

Example: A picture plan can show a helper, a safe place, and the action of moving away from hazards.

Try it together: Sort the plan into three steps and ask an adult to check the sequence.

My independent picture challenge: Draw a safe-help plan using three big symbols.

Caregiver teaching note: Only caregivers approve real safety plans; exclude private addresses and phone numbers.

My evidence: Make a labeled picture or describe a safe decision; have a caregiver review it.

Day 2: Hazard Picture Sorting

Sort picture cards into ask-for-help and stay-away categories; no actual dangerous items.

Example: A paper card showing broken glass belongs in the 'stay away and tell an adult' group.

Try it together: Sort six imaginary hazard pictures into safe practice and adult-help-needed groups.

My independent picture challenge: Draw a two-column safety sorting sheet with labels.

Caregiver teaching note: Do not handle actual sharp objects, chemicals or dangerous materials.

My evidence: Make a labeled picture or describe a safe decision; have a caregiver review it.

Day 3: Caring for Feelings

Practice a calm breathing picture and contacting a trusted adult; people experience emergencies differently.

Example: Some children feel frightened or overwhelmed in a pretend situation. Drawing, slow breathing and a trusted adult may offer support.

Try it together: Match three feelings cards with respectful support choices and an ask-for-help sign.

My independent picture challenge: Draw a calm picture with an adult-help symbol.

Caregiver teaching note: Support accessible communication; calming does not replace immediate help in genuine danger.

My evidence: Make a labeled picture or describe a safe decision; have a caregiver review it.

Day 4: Review and Improve

Ask a caregiver whether pictures are clear, local information is correct and the plan is age-safe. Make one change.

Example: A reviewer might notice that the helper symbol is too small. Making it larger can improve clarity.

Try it together: Ask a trusted adult to review a fictional help plan and suggest one concrete improvement.

My independent picture challenge: Draw before-and-after versions of the same safety card.

Caregiver teaching note: Check local guidance and keep identifying family information private.

My evidence: Make a labeled picture or describe a safe decision; have a caregiver review it.

Day 5: Emergency Awareness Gallery

Present five evidence pictures and a final safe-help plan; the course does not teach independent emergency response.

Example: A gallery of help cards and reflection drawings demonstrates safety awareness, not rescue qualifications.

Try it together: Practice describing one hazard to avoid, one helper role and one safe choice.

My independent picture challenge: Make a portfolio cover with Academy name, lesson, date, title and reflection.

Caregiver teaching note: Celebrate seeking help; never treat quiz results as permission for independent emergency response.

My evidence: Make a labeled picture or describe a safe decision; have a caregiver review it.
