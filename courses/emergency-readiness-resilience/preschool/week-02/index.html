DAY 6 · UNIT 02 · LESSON 06

🚪 Follow a Trusted Adult

🚪 👨‍👩‍👧 🏠

Sometimes an adult decides everyone should leave a place because it is unsafe. Children stay with trusted caregivers, listen, and follow a route the adult has checked. Different hazards need different responses; never assume you should go outside. This is a picture rehearsal, not a real evacuation or a practice alarm.

Try together: Arrange picture cards: find an adult, listen to instructions, and stay together on the adult-chosen route.

Talk together: Who decides whether to leave or stay inside?

DAY 7 · UNIT 02 · LESSON 07

🏠 Staying Safe Indoors

🏠 💛 🪟

Sometimes a responsible adult decides it is safer to remain indoors. This is called sheltering. The right room and action depend on the hazard, local instructions, and each person's needs. We never lock children in rooms or stage dangerous weather. The child's job is to tell a trusted adult and follow their guidance.

Try together: Choose a drawing of a child staying with a caregiver indoors and describe how to ask for help.

Talk together: Why is a caregiver's guidance important for sheltering?

DAY 8 · UNIT 02 · LESSON 08

🦻 Everyone Needs a Way to Communicate

🦻 💬 🧩

Some people speak, sign, use pictures, or use devices to communicate. Emergency plans should consider children with mobility, hearing, sight, sensory or language needs. A family can practice pointing to a help card without sharing phone numbers or home addresses online. Always ask an adult for assistance rather than navigating hazards alone.

Try together: Make a large help picture card or practice a gesture, and show it to a trusted adult.

Talk together: How could you ask for help without speaking?

DAY 9 · UNIT 02 · LESSON 09

🎒 A Grown-Up's Ready Bag

🎒 💧 🧸

Caregivers may prepare emergency supplies such as water, food, a light, necessary personal items, and important information. Needs differ by location and household, including infants, pets and disability supports. Children do not pack or use medicine, sharp tools, batteries, or emergency equipment alone; picture sorting is our entire activity.

Try together: Sort drawings of water, a light, a comfort toy, and medicine into items to discuss with a caregiver; identify adult-only items.

Talk together: Who checks that an emergency supply bag is safe?

DAY 10 · UNIT 02 · LESSON 10

🐾 Caring for People and Pets Safely

🐾 🧑‍🤝‍🧑 💛

Pets are part of many families' emergency plans. A caregiver decides how animals are transported and cared for; children must never re-enter danger or chase a frightened animal. In a pretend story, we stay with our adult and tell them where a pet was last seen. A family plan can include a pet picture without personal contact details.

Try together: Make a picture plan with a caregiver, a safe meeting spot, and optionally a pet; explain staying together.

Talk together: Why should a child never go back into danger for a pet or toy?
