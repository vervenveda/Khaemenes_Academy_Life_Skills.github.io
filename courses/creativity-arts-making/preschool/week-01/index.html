DAY 1 · UNIT 01 · LESSON 01

🖍️ Every Mark Is Art

🖍️ ➰ 🔵

An artist can use lines, dots and shapes to share an idea. A wavy line can suggest a river and a round shape can become a sun. There is no single right way to draw a feeling. You can point to a picture or describe the idea instead of holding a crayon.

Try together: On paper or in the air, make three different pretend lines. Name or describe one.

Talk together: Which mark would you choose to show happiness?

DAY 2 · UNIT 01 · LESSON 02

👀 Look, Notice, Imagine

👀 🟡 🟦

Artists look carefully before creating. We notice big and small shapes, patterns and colors. Imagination lets us make something new from what we see. We respect other people's ideas, and we ask before touching their creations.

Try together: Choose a safe household object and draw, point to, or describe two shapes you notice.

Talk together: How would you change a shape in a make-believe picture?

DAY 3 · UNIT 01 · LESSON 03

🔺 Shape Builders

🔺 🟦 🟠

A circle is round, a triangle has three sides and a square has four equal sides. Artists combine shapes to make houses, animals and imaginary places. Paper shapes or on-screen pictures work well. Small pieces are not suitable for children who might put them in their mouths.

Try together: Arrange three large paper shapes, or point to pictures, to invent a friendly creature.

Talk together: Which shape did you use first, and why?

DAY 4 · UNIT 01 · LESSON 04

🎨 Color Adventures

🔵 🟡 🟢

Colors can help artists tell stories. Red, yellow and blue are familiar paint colors; combining physical paints can produce new colors. Digital colors behave differently from paints. We can predict a mix using pictures without handling substances. A grown-up approves non-toxic materials and keeps supplies away from mouths.

Try together: Predict what happens when blue and yellow paint are mixed, then draw or point to a green result picture.

Talk together: How might you make your picture feel peaceful or energetic?

DAY 5 · UNIT 01 · LESSON 05

📖 My Picture Story

📖 🌈 🖼️

A picture can have a beginning, middle and end. Artists choose colors, marks and shapes to help people understand what happens. We may tell the story aloud, point to pictures, or use words with a grown-up. We respect differences in art and never call another child's creation bad.

Try together: Create or describe three scenes about a helpful imaginary friend. Ask an adult to record your words.

Talk together: What happens first, next and last?
