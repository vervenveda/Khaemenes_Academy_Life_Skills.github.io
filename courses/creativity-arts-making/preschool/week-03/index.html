DAY 11 · UNIT 03 · LESSON 11

💡 My Big Little Idea

💡 🖍️ 🌈

Artists imagine things that matter to them. Our final project can be a picture, paper collage, shape story, helpful sign or make-believe object. A drawing or spoken description is a complete alternative when materials or movement are limited. A grown-up helps us pick a safe, affordable project and writes down our ideas.

Try together: Choose your project; name its purpose and draw or explain three parts.

Talk together: Who might enjoy or benefit from your creation?

DAY 12 · UNIT 03 · LESSON 12

🧩 First Draft, First Try

🧩 📄 ✏️

A draft is the first version of a creative idea. It can be messy, simple or unfinished; it does not have to look like anyone else's work. We can arrange large paper shapes, draw lines or dictate a story. Adults prepare all materials, especially anything requiring cutting or fastening.

Try together: Make a safe first drawing or paper model. Record one choice you made.

Talk together: What does your first draft already show well?

DAY 13 · UNIT 03 · LESSON 13

👂 Kind Feedback, New Ideas

👂 💬 💛

Feedback tells us what someone notices and what we might try next. Helpful words sound like 'I noticed your bright circles' or 'Could the label be bigger?' We ask before changing another person's work. The artist decides, with adult safety support, which suggestions suit their idea.

Try together: Show your draft to a trusted adult and record one kind comment and one possible change.

Talk together: How can you give someone feedback without hurting feelings?

DAY 14 · UNIT 03 · LESSON 14

🔄 Improve and Prepare

🔄 🖼️ 🌟

Revision means choosing a change to make a creation clearer, safer or more useful. A bigger symbol, a repeated pattern or a simpler story order may help. We do not need to spend money or use risky supplies. We can revise by describing a change to our drawing or choosing another picture.

Try together: Show your first idea and an improved idea; explain what changed.

Talk together: Why did you make that change?

DAY 15 · UNIT 03 · LESSON 15

🌟 The Little Makers Showcase

🌟 🎨 🏅

Your work deserves to be heard and seen in a comfortable way. You may present a drawing, tell a story, point to a model or have a caregiver describe your ideas with your permission. Explain what you made, what you improved and one safety rule. A grown-up records observed skills; the final knowledge check and independent practical review remain separate.

Try together: Present your original creation or accessible alternative, discuss three design choices, and ask a grown-up to document observations.

Talk together: What are you proud to share, and what might you explore next?
