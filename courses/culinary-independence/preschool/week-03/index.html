WEEK 3 · DAY 1 · LESSON 11

📋 Plan a Kitchen Helper Project

📋 ✨ Choose a safe project

A project is a small job we think about, practice and finish. We can plan a pretend picnic tray or a real no-heat snack with help. First we decide what to make; then we list what we need and who will help. We can use pictures if we cannot write words yet. Planning helps us notice safety before we begin.

Try with your grown-up: Draw a pretend snack or family table plan with three items and name the adult who will help.

Printable evidence: Keep the picture plan and ask the grown-up to sign today's observation.

Talk together: What do we do before starting a project?

WEEK 3 · DAY 2 · LESSON 12

🧼 Get Our Space Ready

🧼 ✨ Wash hands thoroughly

Before food preparation, we wash hands with soap and water, clean an appropriate surface with adult help, and collect safe supplies. We keep cleaning chemicals away from food. A grown-up handles sanitizing products and chooses safe utensils. Organizing a space helps everyone understand where supplies belong and how to work calmly.

Try with your grown-up: With an adult, practice handwashing and arrange an empty bowl, paper napkins and safe cups on a clean surface.

Printable evidence: Draw your ready-to-work area and describe where supplies were placed.

Talk together: Who may use cleaning chemicals?

WEEK 3 · DAY 3 · LESSON 13

🍇 Build the Family Snack or Pretend Tray

🍇 ✨ Look at your plan

Making our plan real means following one instruction at a time. A grown-up prepares any food that requires cutting, cooking or special texture changes. The child may set out napkins, count cups, name ingredients or arrange pretend-food cards. Different households choose different foods, so a project can be adjusted for allergies, budgets and availability.

Try with your grown-up: Help assemble a no-heat snack approved by the caregiver, or arrange a pretend paper-food tray. Follow the adult's instructions throughout.

Printable evidence: Draw the finished tray. Record which tasks the child completed and which the adult did.

Talk together: What should we do if an ingredient is unsafe?

WEEK 3 · DAY 4 · LESSON 14

🤝 Practice Sharing and Cleaning Up

🤝 ✨ Invite someone politely

Helping is more than making something. We practice offering, waiting our turn, listening and thanking people. Cleaning up includes returning safe supplies to their places and washing hands when necessary. An adult manages sharp tools, electrical equipment, hot surfaces and cleaning products. We can feel proud of small acts of teamwork.

Try with your grown-up: Practice offering your pretend tray to a family member, asking politely, and putting away child-safe supplies with supervision.

Printable evidence: Draw a helper action and record a kind phrase used during practice.

Talk together: How can you ask someone to share kindly?

WEEK 3 · DAY 5 · LESSON 15

🌟 Our Kitchen Helper Celebration

🌟 ✨ Choose your favorite work

We learned to notice safety, ask for help, wash hands, recognize safe tools, count, sort, plan, share and clean up. We can explain a favorite lesson and show a grown-up the pictures and notes we made. Learning a skill takes practice. A sticker celebrates effort, while our teacher or caregiver checks what we truly know and can do safely.

Try with your grown-up: Show your drawings and project to a caregiver. Explain three kitchen safety ideas and demonstrate a safe helper task under supervision.

Printable evidence: Build the five-day portfolio, add a caregiver's practical observation and complete the knowledge assessment.

Talk together: Which safe kitchen skill would you like to practice again?
