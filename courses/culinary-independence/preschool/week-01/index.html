Welcome, Little Kitchen Helper!

We will learn about kitchens, clean hands, ingredients, measuring and being a good helper. Each day has a short original story-like lesson, a safe activity, and a printable drawing prompt. Your grown-up will help you.

Caregiver safety: No independent use of heat, knives, cleaning chemicals or risky foods. Check allergies, age-specific choking hazards and household needs. Supervise any real food preparation. Picture-based simulations are always acceptable practice, but are not proof of physical competence.

Learning goals

By the end of the week, learners can name a safe helper job, describe why we wash hands, identify familiar ingredients with an adult, explore measurements, and ask for help before a risky activity.

DAY 1 · Unit 01 · Lesson 01

My Kitchen Is a Helping Place

A kitchen is where families prepare and share food. Some things are safe to look at but not safe to touch. A grown-up helps us decide which jobs are right for us. We can be helpful by listening, naming foods, setting out napkins, and asking before we begin.

Let's explore: Look at a kitchen picture or stand near a safe preparation area with a caregiver. Name three things you see. Point to the place where you wash hands.

Printable evidence: Draw a helpful kitchen scene. Circle one job a grown-up helps you do.

Talk together: What is one safe way you can help?

DAY 2 · Unit 01 · Lesson 02

Clean Hands, Happy Helpers

Our hands touch toys, tables, pets and many other things. Before we help prepare food, we wash with soap and running water. Rub palms, backs of hands, between fingers and around nails, rinse well, and dry using a clean towel. Ask an adult to help you reach the sink.

Let's explore: With an adult, practice careful handwashing. Pretend to prepare food only after your hands are clean.

Printable evidence: Trace your hands and decorate the drawing with bubbles. Ask your grown-up to write what you practiced.

Talk together: When should we wash our hands before helping with food?

DAY 3 · Unit 01 · Lesson 03

Colors, Shapes & Ingredients

Fruits, vegetables, grains and other ingredients have different colors, shapes and textures. Looking closely helps us recognize foods and talk about what we might eat. We do not taste unknown foods or ingredients without permission; some people have allergies, and small or hard foods can be choking hazards.

Let's explore: With your grown-up, sort picture cards or drawings of familiar foods by color, shape or food group. Ask whether any foods are unsafe for your family.

Printable evidence: Draw or name three familiar ingredients. Describe their colors or shapes.

Talk together: What should you ask before tasting a food?

DAY 4 · Unit 01 · Lesson 04

Measure, Count & Share

A recipe is a set of instructions. Measuring helps us use the right amount. We can practice with empty measuring cups, dry large safe pretend pieces or water with an adult. Counting, scooping and noticing full and empty connect cooking to mathematics. Never pour or handle hot liquids.

Let's explore: Place empty cups in order from small to large. With an adult, count three pretend scoops or safely measure cool water.

Printable evidence: Draw three scoops or three cups. Tell the grown-up which container holds more.

Talk together: Why do recipes tell us how much to use?

DAY 5 · Unit 01 · Lesson 05

Our Family Kitchen Celebration

We can put our learning together: ask permission, wash hands, check that food is suitable, choose a safe job, and clean up together. The goal is to be a thoughtful helper, not to cook independently. A grown-up checks practical safety; our picture questions show what we understand.

Let's explore: With a caregiver, plan a no-heat pretend picnic or safe snack. Choose an age-appropriate task, describe your steps, and share your family kitchen story.

Printable evidence: Draw your kitchen celebration. Ask a caregiver to note one skill you demonstrated and one thing to keep practicing.

Talk together: What will you remember next time you help?
