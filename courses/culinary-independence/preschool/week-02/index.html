WEEK 2 · DAY 1 · LESSON 06

🥣 Find Our Kitchen Tools

🥣 ✨ Look at the safe tools

A bowl holds ingredients while we mix. A spoon can stir soft foods. A measuring cup helps us compare amounts. Every tool has a job, and our grown-up chooses which tools are safe to use. Knives, hot pans, ovens and electrical machines are grown-up tools. We can learn by naming and observing without touching them.

Try with your grown-up: Put a clean bowl, a spoon and a measuring cup on a table with an adult. Point to each. Ask: Which tool holds food? Which one stirs?

Printable evidence: Draw a bowl and spoon. Explain how each helps.

Talk together: What should we do before taking a tool?

WEEK 2 · DAY 2 · LESSON 07

🥄 Count and Measure

🥄 ✨ Find two safe containers

Measuring means noticing how much of something we have. We can count one, two, three pretend scoops into a bowl and compare a small cup with a large cup. The size of a cup changes how much it holds. A grown-up can show water measurement safely over a tray. We never taste ingredients without permission.

Try with your grown-up: With safe empty cups, count three pretend scoops. Tell the grown-up which cup is bigger and which is smaller.

Printable evidence: Make three circles for three scoops and mark the larger cup.

Talk together: Why should we ask before pouring water?

WEEK 2 · DAY 3 · LESSON 08

🍓 Mix, Stir, and Notice

🍓 ✨ Ask the grown-up for a safe bowl

Stirring can bring ingredients together. A slow circular movement is often enough for soft foods. We keep the bowl steady on a safe surface while a grown-up supervises. We can notice texture: some mixtures are smooth and others have pieces. We do not stir hot food or operate mixers by ourselves.

Try with your grown-up: Stir a small bowl of cooled, ready-to-eat food approved by your grown-up, or pretend with paper circles and an empty spoon.

Printable evidence: Draw a before-and-after bowl and say what changed.

Talk together: How does stirring change the things in a bowl?

WEEK 2 · DAY 4 · LESSON 09

🌈 Sort and Choose Food

🌈 ✨ Look at food picture cards

Foods come in many colors, shapes and textures. Some foods grow on plants, while others come from different sources. We can make groups by color or type without saying that only one color is healthy. Fruits and vegetables can be enjoyed in different ways. Ask a grown-up about allergies, washing and safe serving sizes.

Try with your grown-up: Use food picture cards to create two groups by color; ask a grown-up if each picture has been placed thoughtfully.

Printable evidence: Make a drawing of two food groups with names or color dots.

Talk together: Can the same food belong in two different groups?

WEEK 2 · DAY 5 · LESSON 10

🧺 Make a Safe Snack Plan

🧺 ✨ Wash hands with help

A snack plan begins with clean hands, a safe workspace and a caregiver who knows about allergies. We choose foods that are already safely prepared or that an adult prepares for us. Preschool helpers might arrange napkins or name ingredients. We always sit while eating, and food textures and sizes must be suitable for the child's age and ability.

Try with your grown-up: With an adult, plan a simple ready-to-eat snack or a pretend snack tray. Do not handle choking-hazard foods without appropriate adult preparation.

Printable evidence: Draw the snack tray, name two safe helper jobs, and write a caregiver observation.

Talk together: What two things should happen before you eat?
