DAY 1 · UNIT 01 · LESSON 01

🏠 My Home Has Helping Places

🏠 🌈 ✨

A home has places where people rest, eat, play, learn and care for one another. Every family home can look different. A child can help by finding where safe belongings belong. We ask before moving someone else's things. Adults decide what is safe to touch.

Family practice: Find three familiar places with a trusted adult and draw one. Name a safe helping job.

Talk together: Where do your toys belong?

DAY 2 · UNIT 01 · LESSON 02

🧸 Put Away, Find Again

🧸 🌈 ✨

Putting something away means returning it to a known place. We can sort toys, books and soft items into labeled bins with pictures. An organized play area makes it easier to find things later. A grown-up manages heavy furniture, sharp items and anything electrical.

Family practice: Put away five child-safe objects and draw a picture label for their container.

Talk together: How does tidying help us?

DAY 3 · UNIT 01 · LESSON 03

🫧 Clean or Not Clean?

🫧 🌈 ✨

Cleaning means removing messes, but young children must not handle household chemicals, sprays or disinfectants. We can wipe a safe dry table with an adult-approved cloth, place laundry in a basket, and ask for help with spills. We wash hands when our caregiver says it is needed.

Family practice: With supervision, tidy a pretend table using a clean dry cloth and describe which products are adult-only.

Talk together: What should we do if we see a bottle of cleaner?

DAY 4 · UNIT 01 · LESSON 04

🧺 Laundry Color Detectives

🧺 🌈 ✨

Laundry includes clothes, towels and other washable fabric. People sometimes sort laundry by color, fabric or washing directions. Preschool children can match pictures or pair clean socks, but a grown-up operates the washing machine and handles detergents. We never climb inside machines.

Family practice: Match three pairs of clean socks or paper sock drawings by color or pattern.

Talk together: Who uses the washing machine?

DAY 5 · UNIT 01 · LESSON 05

🌟 Our Family Helper Plan

🌟 🌈 ✨

A simple routine is something we practice often, such as putting books away before bedtime or placing shoes in a safe spot. A family can choose one realistic helper habit together. We do not need a perfect room; we need a plan that is kind, safe and suited to our abilities. Practice is something to celebrate.

Family practice: Create a picture routine for one safe household job, practice it, and collect a caregiver observation.

Talk together: How can we help without touching unsafe items?
