WEEK 3 · DAY 1 · LESSON 11

🗂️ Choose a Family Helper Project

🗂️ 🏠 ✨

A project is something we plan, practice and finish. We can make a picture chart for putting away toys, design a clean book corner or plan where safe supplies belong. First we talk about what is helpful and which tasks are safe. Family members may have different needs, so our plan should include everyone kindly.

Try together: Draw one small home improvement you would like to make together.

Talk together: Who will help you with your project?

WEEK 3 · DAY 2 · LESSON 12

🎨 Gather Safe Supplies

🎨 🏠 ✨

Before beginning, helpers check their space. Paper, crayons, picture labels and empty containers may help us organize. We never use cleaning chemicals, sharp tools, heavy boxes or electrical equipment without an adult doing the hazardous work. We can use pretend supplies when something is not available.

Try together: Set out safe paper labels or picture cards with an adult.

Talk together: What would you use for your project?

WEEK 3 · DAY 3 · LESSON 13

🖍️ Create Our Helping Chart

🖍️ 🏠 ✨

A helping chart shows when or where a safe job happens. Pictures can stand for toys, books, clean socks or napkins. We can count tasks, name colors and put steps in order. Children and families may choose different kinds of homes and routines. A helper chart reminds us; it is never a reason to shame someone who needs support.

Try together: Make a three-picture helper chart with a grown-up.

Talk together: What does each picture tell us to do?

WEEK 3 · DAY 4 · LESSON 14

🔄 Try It and Make It Better

🔄 🏠 ✨

When we try a plan, we learn whether the steps are clear. If a basket is too high or a task is too hard, we can change the plan instead of forcing it. Adults help decide what is safe and accessible. We ask questions, listen to feedback and celebrate small improvements.

Try together: Follow your helper chart and ask what should change.

Talk together: What would make your chart easier?

WEEK 3 · DAY 5 · LESSON 15

🌟 Present Our Happy Home Project

🌟 🏠 ✨

Our project is ready to share. We can show the before-and-after drawings, explain three safe helping actions and thank the people who helped. A grown-up can describe which tasks they directly observed. Finishing a game gives a practice sticker; the final knowledge check and practical review are separate.

Try together: Show your chart or pretend project, explain three safety ideas and demonstrate one approved job.

Talk together: What do you feel proud of learning?
