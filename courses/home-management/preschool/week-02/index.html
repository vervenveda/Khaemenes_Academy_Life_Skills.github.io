WEEK 2 · DAY 1 · LESSON 6

🌞 My Morning Helper Routine

🌞 🏠 ✨

A routine is a set of steps we practice in order. We wake up, wash hands or face as needed, choose clothes with help and put belongings where they belong. Families have different schedules, and a picture plan helps us remember without rushing. Adults help with water temperature and personal-care products.

Try together: Make a three-picture morning routine with your grown-up.

Talk together: What do you do before breakfast?

WEEK 2 · DAY 2 · LESSON 7

🧦 Laundry Colors and Care

🧦 🏠 ✨

Clothes may have different colors, sizes and care instructions. A child can match clean socks or sort clothes by color with supervision. Laundry products are chemicals and machines have hazards. Grown-ups handle detergent, washing machines, dryers, cords and hot ironing equipment.

Try together: Sort four clean socks into pairs or color groups.

Talk together: Who uses detergent and laundry machines?

WEEK 2 · DAY 3 · LESSON 8

🛏️ Set a Cozy Rest Space

🛏️ 🏠 ✨

A calm rest space helps us settle. We can arrange a favorite book or place a small toy on a shelf, if our grown-up says it is safe. A space can be tidy even when it is shared with siblings. Adults check bedding and furniture safety; children must not climb furniture or move heavy objects.

Try together: Choose two safe items to return to their places in your rest area.

Talk together: What helps you feel ready for rest?

WEEK 2 · DAY 4 · LESSON 9

🧹 Care for Our Shared Spaces

🧹 🏠 ✨

Shared spaces belong to everyone who uses them. We respect other people's belongings and ask before moving them. We can put away crayons, stack picture books, and identify an adult-only spill or broken object. We do not touch broken glass, unknown substances or cleaning liquids.

Try together: Practice a two-minute toy or book tidy-up with your grown-up.

Talk together: What should you do if you find a broken object?

WEEK 2 · DAY 5 · LESSON 10

⏳ My Five-Minute Family Routine

⏳ 🏠 ✨

Small habits grow when we practice them often. A short routine might mean matching socks, putting books away or setting napkins with an adult. We can draw a beginning, middle and end. Completing a routine means practicing and learning; stickers are encouragement, while adults separately assess safe actions.

Try together: Make a three-step picture routine and practice it with supervision.

Talk together: What is one routine you could do again tomorrow?
