DAY 11 · UNIT 03 · LESSON 11

🧰 Imagine a Helpful Role

🧰 🌈 💛

A role explains how someone contributes. A librarian may help visitors find books; caregivers support people in many ways; artists create useful images. Our classroom project lets us imagine helping others without pretending that certain careers belong to one gender or background.

Try together: Choose a community role and draw one way that person helps.

Talk together: What other roles might help the same community?

DAY 12 · UNIT 03 · LESSON 12

🧰 Plan the Pretend Task

🧰 🌈 💛

A good pretend task has a goal, safe materials, ordered steps and a trusted grown-up. Children can use crayons, picture cards and clean paper. We never ask children to operate machinery, handle chemicals or provide unpaid services for adults as a lesson requirement.

Try together: Draw three picture steps for an imaginary library helper or garden guide.

Talk together: What should a caregiver check before practice begins?

DAY 13 · UNIT 03 · LESSON 13

🧰 Try and Observe

🧰 🌈 💛

We learn by trying a safe version of our plan and noticing what works. A pretend library helper can sort drawn book covers; a garden guide can arrange picture labels. Describe what happened honestly rather than claiming a task was performed in the real world when it was only imagined.

Try together: Act out the task with paper cards, then draw one thing that worked.

Talk together: What is the difference between pretending and observing a real action?

DAY 14 · UNIT 03 · LESSON 14

🧰 Revise With Feedback

🧰 🌈 💛

Kind feedback can make an idea more helpful. A sign may need bigger letters, a picture may need clearer meaning or a task may need a rest break. Changes are a normal part of learning. Respect suggestions without allowing criticism to become teasing.

Try together: Ask a caregiver for one suggestion and revise your picture plan.

Talk together: What change made the project more accessible or clear?

DAY 15 · UNIT 03 · LESSON 15

🧰 My Future Helper Showcase

🧰 🌈 💛

Present your pretend role through pictures, speech, gestures or a supported communication device. Explain what the helper does, why it matters, how you stayed safe and one improvement you made. A caregiver documents the demonstration separately from the 80% knowledge check.

Try together: Show your three-step pretend-work project privately and record caregiver feedback.

Talk together: How could different strengths help people cooperate?
