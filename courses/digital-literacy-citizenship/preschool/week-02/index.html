DAY 6 · UNIT 02 · LESSON 06

💻 Pictures Can Tell Stories

💻 🌈 💛

We can tell stories with pictures, shapes and symbols. A picture made on paper is still creative digital-literacy practice. If a picture shows someone else, ask permission before sharing it. Keep projects private unless a caregiver agrees.

Try together: Tell a three-picture story without photographing or uploading it.

Talk together: When should we ask before sharing a picture?

DAY 7 · UNIT 02 · LESSON 07

💻 Not Everything Online Is True

💻 🌈 💛

Not every image or claim shown on a screen is accurate. Some pictures are pretend or edited. When something surprising appears, ask a caregiver to help check it. Preschool learners need guidance rather than independent internet research.

Try together: Compare a pretend dragon picture with a real animal photograph and explain which needs checking.

Talk together: Who can help check a surprising claim?

DAY 8 · UNIT 02 · LESSON 08

💻 Advertisements Want Attention

💻 🌈 💛

An advertisement tries to attract attention to products or services. Bright colors, prizes and buy buttons do not mean we should purchase anything. A caregiver decides whether a purchase is appropriate. We can learn to recognize pretend advertisements without visiting stores.

Try together: Circle the pretend 'Buy Now' sign on a paper advertisement and ask an adult before purchase.

Talk together: Does a colorful advertisement mean we have to buy something?

DAY 9 · UNIT 02 · LESSON 09

💻 Ask Before Sharing Photos

💻 🌈 💛

Before posting or sending a picture, ask the people shown and a responsible adult. Family photos, school names and locations can reveal private details. It is fine to keep a drawing entirely offline. Sharing is never required to finish a lesson.

Try together: Choose whether to keep a friend's picture private until permission is given.

Talk together: How does permission protect other people?

DAY 10 · UNIT 02 · LESSON 10

💻 Safe Digital Helpers

💻 🌈 💛

If a game, message or picture feels confusing or unsafe, children can stop, move away and tell a trusted adult. Adults can help close a page or choose a different activity. We never need to click unknown links or contact strangers to solve a digital problem.

Try together: Practice stop, close and tell using a pretend paper screen.

Talk together: Who helps when a digital activity feels unsafe?
