DAY 11 · UNIT 03 · LESSON 11

💻 Design an Offline Poster

💻 🌈 💛

A poster can remind us of digital kindness and privacy. We can draw a large stop symbol, a kind speech bubble, and an ask-an-adult sign. Paper posters are free and do not require a device. Our project is a learning tool, not something that needs to be posted online.

Try together: Create a paper poster with kind words, privacy and ask-an-adult pictures.

Talk together: Which picture on your poster reminds you to ask an adult?

DAY 12 · UNIT 03 · LESSON 12

💻 Choose Healthy Screen Habits

💻 🌈 💛

Healthy routines balance screens with sleep, conversation, imaginative play and accessible movement. Every family's schedule differs, so a caregiver helps choose a realistic plan. If a child uses assistive technology, accessible needs come first.

Try together: Arrange picture cards for rest, play, device use and sleep with caregiver guidance.

Talk together: What screen-free activity do you enjoy?

DAY 13 · UNIT 03 · LESSON 13

💻 Keep Information Private

💻 🌈 💛

A digital form might ask for a name, address, photo or password. Preschool children should not fill in real forms independently. Use pretend details on a paper example and ask an adult when information is requested. We never need to disclose private data to earn a badge.

Try together: Cross out a pretend request for a real password on a paper form.

Talk together: Why should passwords remain private?

DAY 14 · UNIT 03 · LESSON 14

💻 Revise My Digital Rules

💻 🌈 💛

Good plans can change after kind feedback. Maybe the reminder sign is hard to see, or a routine needs more rest time. A caregiver can help enlarge pictures, use familiar words and create accessible alternatives. Improving a plan is learning, not failure.

Try together: Ask a caregiver to review your poster and change one detail for clarity.

Talk together: What change made your plan clearer?

DAY 15 · UNIT 03 · LESSON 15

💻 My Digital Citizenship Showcase

💻 🌈 💛

Present a paper digital-citizenship poster to a caregiver. Explain one kind-message choice, one private detail not to share and one way to ask for help. A caregiver observes the demonstration separately from the knowledge quiz; local records do not establish verified credentials.

Try together: Present your poster and explain three digital-safety decisions.

Talk together: What will you remember about being kind and careful online?
