DAY 1 · UNIT 01 · LESSON 01

💻 Screens Are Tools

💻 🌈 💛

A screen can display stories, drawings and learning games. Screens are tools, not people who can always tell us what is true. Children ask their caregivers which activities are right for their age. Drawing on paper teaches the same storytelling skills without a device.

Try together: Sort pictures of drawing, video calling a caregiver and resting into screen and offline activities.

Talk together: How might a screen help us learn without replacing real-world play?

DAY 2 · UNIT 01 · LESSON 02

💻 Ask Before Going Online

💻 🌈 💛

Before opening a game or website, pause and ask a trusted adult. Some buttons lead to unknown places or ask for personal details. Never tap a pop-up to win a pretend prize without permission. A paper drawing of a device is enough to practice.

Try together: Point to a pretend unknown link and practice saying 'Ask an adult first.'

Talk together: What should we do before opening an unfamiliar link?

DAY 3 · UNIT 01 · LESSON 03

💻 My Private Information

💻 🌈 💛

A password helps protect accounts. Your home address, family phone numbers, personal photos and passwords are private information. A child should not type or share these details for a classroom game. Trusted adults choose safe ways to handle information.

Try together: Sort pretend password, house number and colored shape cards into private or safe-to-share examples.

Talk together: Which details should stay private?

DAY 4 · UNIT 01 · LESSON 04

💻 Kind Digital Words

💻 🌈 💛

Words on a screen can make people feel welcome or hurt. We can use kind greetings and avoid teasing. If a message is frightening or unkind, stop and tell a trusted adult. Nobody must answer upsetting messages alone.

Try together: Choose a kind reply to a pretend message without sending anything online.

Talk together: How can words show kindness?

DAY 5 · UNIT 01 · LESSON 05

💻 Take Screen Breaks

💻 🌈 💛

Eyes and bodies benefit from rest, movement and other activities. Families choose routines appropriate to children's needs. A quiet screen break might include drawing, conversation or accessible movement. We do not need a timer or paid app to practice.

Try together: Draw a two-part plan with screen time and a screen-free activity.

Talk together: What can you enjoy away from a screen?
