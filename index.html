About Life Skills & Practical Independence

The Life Skills branch is the canonical curriculum home for sixteen practical-learning pathways, including culinary independence, home management, gardening, creativity and making, entrepreneurship, personal finance, health and first aid, emergency readiness, digital citizenship, career readiness, transportation, civic living, relationships, sustainability, caregiving, and legal and consumer literacy. Learning progresses by age and stage while preserving practical safety, local control of student work, and real-world evidence of skill.

Program Features

Sixteen Practical Pathways Daily living, food, home care, gardening, finance, work, technology, civic life, relationships, sustainability, caregiving, consumer literacy, creativity, entrepreneurship, health, and emergency readiness form one coherent practical-learning network.
Age-Appropriate Progression Learning grows from supervised exploration and simple routines into multi-step practice, independent projects, adult-level standards, and community-ready portfolios without forcing one maturity level across every learner.
Safety & Real-World Demonstration Cooking, tools, first aid, cleaning, gardening, transportation, caregiving, and emergency skills require clear safety boundaries and appropriate adult supervision. Practical competence is demonstrated separately from written knowledge.
Mastery, Evidence & Independence Complete courses use visible lessons, offline reading and practice, accessible printables, authentic demonstrations, reflection, documented evidence, and an 80% knowledge mastery gate while student work remains local unless intentionally exported.
